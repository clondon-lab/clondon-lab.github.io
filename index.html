Energy Transfer (NYSE: ET)

Fall 2026

Energy Transfer is an American midstream energy infrastructure company with approximately 140,000 miles of pipeline across 44 states. ET has a high quality of earnings, with roughly 90% of 2026 adjusted EBITDA expected to be fee-based. Its Q2 2026 Adjusted EBITDA was just over $5B, up 31%, with a $74B market cap. ET also delivers a high 6.3% distribution yield, a payout it has raised for 19 consecutive quarters. Energy Transfer is fundamentally solid and set for significant growth as liquefied natural gas and data center energy demand continue to grow. Investors are overweighting the risks of high leverage and an acquisitive growth strategy, and are not pricing in enough ET's scale advantage in meeting that demand.

Energy Transfer's pipelines run directly from wellhead to demand center, creating a high switching cost for their customers. Furthermore, their large contracts frequently run 10 to 20 years, much of it firm capacity. Shippers effectively rent the pipe rather than paying for what they actually move, which shields ET from energy cyclicity.

Energy Transfer has a large moat and is well protected from new entrants. An interstate line needs a FERC certificate that often takes over six years to secure, not to mention the billions of dollars and time needed to build it. ET also owns the general partner interests in USA Compression Partners and Sunoco, vertically integrating its own compression and fuel distribution and adding depth against peers like Kinder Morgan, Enterprise Products Partners, and Williams.

Looking forward, Energy Transfer's management sees around 16 billion cubic feet per day (Bcf/d) of LNG demand growth by the early 2030s. Not to mention the demand already apparent from data centers. To meet it, ET is spending $5.6B to $5.9B of growth capex this year and expects $5B or more annually through 2029. Hugh Brinson Phase I is in service and reaching its full 1.5 Bcf/d this month, and the $5.6B Desert Southwest Pipeline will add 2.3 Bcf/d by late 2029. Management is targeting 3-5% annual distribution growth.

ET's biggest threats are its aggressive buildout strategy and its corporate structure. Their leverage is targeted at 4-4.5x EBITDA and currently sits in the lower half of that range, supporting BBB/Baa2 ratings. The markets have factored in this sizeable number, but Q2 distributable cash flow covered the distribution more than twice over, a safety net for this level of leverage. There is demonstrated need for the pipelines they are building, and regardless of whether data center buildout is excessive, those facilities will require immense energy to run.

As for their corporate structure, ET is an MLP meaning investors receive a K-1 instead of 1099. This repels index funds and tax-exempt institutions and causes ET to trade at a cheaper price. My thesis does not need that discount to close. Instead, the return comes from the 6.3% distribution and 3-5% growth on top of it from purely expanding operations. Many investors are also afraid of ET's aggressive acquisitions and capex spend, but I believe now is the time for that more than ever. Long-haul capacity gets contracted once and for a decade, so whoever builds the infrastructure first owns it- ET's management has made it clear that they intend to be a winner. Management has also demonstrated restraint, such as suspending Lake Charles LNG in December when it could not sell down the stake it wanted, redirecting that capital to a pipeline backlog with better risk-adjusted returns.
